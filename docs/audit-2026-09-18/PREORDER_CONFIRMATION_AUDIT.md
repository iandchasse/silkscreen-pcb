## 3. The four follow-up research questions

### C9 (frontlight boost output capacitor) — raise to 4.7 µF, does not hurt flicker

TI's TPS923610 datasheet recommends 1–4.7 µF for COUT, and its own dimming architecture chops the *internal reference*, low-pass-filtered before it reaches the current-control loop — so there is no PWM-frequency current edge at the LED string for a bigger output cap to smear. Raising COUT should be neutral-to-beneficial for the PWM/ADIM-based dimming that was "the main goal" of this question.

C9 as populated (1 µF/50 V/0805/X7R) is estimated to derate to ~0.6–0.8 µF effective at the 15–24 V the LED rail actually runs at — below TI's own 1 µF minimum. Recommendation: **4.7 µF/50 V/0805.** LCSC: Samwha `CS2012X5R475K500NRE` (`C513770`) — the same part already used for C11/C13–C17, so no new BOM line. DigiKey-findable prime: TDK `CGA4J1X7R1H475K125AC`.

**One real tradeoff, found while writing this up (not part of the original flicker question):** the board separately uses C9's charge as the RC time constant for the cool/warm LED-string color-blend transition (`docs/HARDWARE.md` §8: `t ≈ C9 × ΔV_f / I_LED`). Raising C9 4.7× proportionally slows that transition ~4.7×. Order-of-magnitude only: roughly 75 µs → 350 µs, almost certainly imperceptible for a CCT blend rather than a display-refresh signal — but blending is already flagged in the docs as unvalidated by board waveforms, so this is a genuine, if probably minor, secondary consideration. Not a reason to withhold the change; worth checking blend timing empirically whenever blending itself gets qualified.

### L1 (EPD high-voltage boost inductor) — applied, 22 µH → 47 µH

The SSD1677 controller datasheet's own reference circuit (§13, Table 13-1) specifies 47 µH + 2.2 Ω — confirming what DESIGN_REVIEW.md could previously only cite from the panel vendor's drawing. The board had run 22 µH + 3 Ω since the first commit, roughly a quarter of the reference design's energy per switching pulse, with no reported refresh or ghosting symptom.

**First survey (same-footprint only) found nothing usable:** the only same-footprint (3.0×3.0×1.2 mm) 47 µH shielded inductor is TDK's `VLS3012CX-470M-1`, DigiKey-only with a ~12-week lead time and worse Isat/DCR than the current part; every in-stock LCSC 47 µH alternative in that size needed a visibly larger, unshielded footprint.

**Once the owner confirmed footprint was not a constraint, several searches converged on a final pair.** LCSC-only, cost-prioritized candidates (SHOU HAN `CYA1040-47UH`, CENKER `CKCS6045`/`CKCS5040`) had excellent LCSC pricing but no DigiKey presence at all. A dual-sourced candidate, TDK `VLS5045EX-470M-H`, was confirmed live on both distributors but didn't beat LCSC-side cost, the owner's stated priority. The owner then sourced a Bourns `SRN5040-470M` — an automated DigiKey read initially reported this as shielded; re-checked directly against Bourns' own datasheet, which titles the part **"Semi-shielded Power Inductors"** — a real error, caught before finalizing. Final part: **Laird `TYS5040470M-10`** (prime/DigiKey — active, direct-sold, 24.2k stock, $0.41/$0.34/$0.28 @1/10/100, shielded per Laird's own datasheet: "magnetic shielded structure") paired with **Sunltech `SLW5040S470MST` / LCSC `C206267`** (1.85k stock, $0.046/pc @qty10 down to $0.024/pc @qty10.5k) — both parts 5.0×5.0×4.0 mm, both genuinely shielded, no shielding or footprint asymmetry between builds.

**This is a footprint change (3.0×3.0 mm → 5.0×5.0 mm), not a drop-in.** L1's nearest neighbors — a 0603 cap, a 0603 resistor, Q4, a SOD-123 diode, all 3–4.4 mm from L1's old center — will need to move. Routing-length figures for the GDR/RESE/EINK_SW nets in DESIGN_REVIEW.md §7 predate this move and need re-measuring once the new layout is placed. **Footprint applied: `Inductor_SMD:L_APV_ANR5040`** (1.4 mm pads, 2.3 mm gap) — checked directly against Bourns' own published land pattern for this case class (1.5 mm pads, 2.1 mm gap): a close, conservative match. A footprint generated for a Cenker part was tried first and rejected — its 1.1 mm pad gap was a full millimeter tighter than Bourns' own recommendation, a real solder-bridging risk during reflow.

**R14: applied 2026-09-18, 3 Ω → 2.2 Ω** (Yageo `RC0603FR-072R2L` / LCSC `C112307`, same 0603 1% series/price class as the prior part). With L1 now genuinely at 47 µH, this lands the pair exactly on the SSD1677 reference operating point. Confirmed to stay within every component rating (peak current +36%, per-pulse energy +86%, still far under the new L1's Isat and Q4's current rating) — but **not bench-verified**; scope GDR/RESE and confirm VGH/VGL/VSH settle within SSD1677 spec on the first boards.

### FS8205A package — a manufacturer mix-up, not an error on either side

This one was worth re-checking directly rather than deferring to either the original finding or the correction. **Both were partly right.** Fortune Semiconductor's own "FS8205A" datasheet — every revision, 2009 through 2016 — is TSSOP-8 only; their SOT-23-6 dual-FET part is named "FS8205," no A. But a genuine, orderable SOT-23-6 "FS8205A" does exist, from at least three other makers: TECH PUBLIC (LCSC `C2830320`, already the JLC-build part), FUXINSEMI (`C908265`), and **EVVOSEMI, which DigiKey stocks directly** (PN `26220994`) — their own datasheet's ordering info literally reads "Equivalent Part Number: FS8205A," package SOT-23-6, and the pinout matches this board's footprint exactly.

So "FS8205A comes in SOT-23" is correct for the generic/clone-ecosystem naming, just not for Fortune specifically. **Fix applied to the markdown docs:** the hand-build sourcing note now points to EVVOSEMI as a DigiKey-findable prime source, not Fortune. `fabrication/part_fields.csv` and `fabrication/BOM_handbuild_digikey.csv` (data files, out of scope for this markdown-only pass) still carry "Fortune Semiconductor" in the Manufacturer field and should be updated to match.

### USB VBUS hot-plug ringing — separate from the sustained-fault question, still worth a first-article check but not urgent

Distinct from §2.5's sustained-overvoltage question, which the owner correctly resolved via the board's Rd/CC behavior: this is about a fast, tens-of-microsecond ring at nominal 5 V during physical connector mate, driven by cable inductance against USB_VBUS's bulk ceramic — unrelated to PD negotiation, and possible with any 5 V source. TI's own TP4056 datasheet names this phenomenon directly and recommends 1–1.5 Ω of series damping for it. A worst-case linear model puts the peak at 6–7.4 V, above TPS2116's 6 V absolute maximum, but the physics need a fairly stiff/fast source and a low-inductance cable simultaneously — real chargers mostly have soft-start behavior the model doesn't credit, and no field issue has been reported on prior boards with a similar front end. **Verdict: low-probability, not something to design around pre-emptively.** Kept as a first-article scope check (probe USB_VBUS at hot-plug with a short, thick A-to-C cable) rather than a design change.

## 4. Battery-path trace width — assessed, owner's call

Checked directly with pcbnew rather than estimated: every segment on B+, B− and P+ has real routing headroom to widen without ripping anything up. Worst-case clearance to a neighboring different-net track is 0.43 mm (P+), 0.56 mm (B−) and 0.60 mm (B+), against the board's 0.20 mm minimum clearance rule — so B+/B− could comfortably go from their current 0.20–0.25 mm to roughly 0.4 mm, and most of P+ similarly (one ~3 mm P+ segment is tighter and would cap a uniform width there).

This only matters for a fault case (something shorting the exposed P+ header pin, or a hard short elsewhere in the path) held just under the DW01A's over-current trip for its ~10–20 ms timer — normal operation at ≤0.5 A is a non-issue at the current width. **Assessment: low-risk, roughly 15–20 minutes of work, no schematic impact — worth doing if you're already in the PCB editor, not worth a dedicated pass on its own.** Left as an optional cleanup; not applied.
